TWO DATA PRODUCTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIM2D  100 x 2200, complete at 446 packets  (standard trimmed product)
  NOPROC  515 x 2200, complete at 2248 packets (full unprocessed detector)

The TRIM2D frame is exactly NOPROC rows 187-286. Established by matching the
fixed hot-pixel pattern between both products of one exposure (Jaccard 0.92 at
offset 187 against 0.29 for the next-best) and confirmed by 188,946 commonly
valid pixels agreeing bit-for-bit.

Visit1 holds TRIM2D only; Visit4 holds both; Visit7 holds NOPROC only.

REJECTION CRITERION  (relaxed, at the users request)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Discard a frame if either:
  (a) max_dist > 5 px   - a missing pixel further than 5 px from any real pixel
  (b) missing > 25%

NOPROC frames are judged on the science region (rows 187-286), not the whole
detector, because that band is the TRIM2D product. Frames 1139 and 1140 look
poor over the full frame - 1140 has an 80-row dead swath, max_dist 83 - while
their science regions are nearly clean (7.4% and 2.5% missing, max_dist 2).

NOTE: these limits are looser than the Uranus set, which used max_dist <= 3 px
and missing <= 20%. They were relaxed deliberately so that Visit7 would retain
more than a single exposure. Ground-truth testing (real loss masks applied to
the complete 446-packet frames, then interpolated and compared with truth)
gives, for the extracted 1-D spectrum:
      3.4% missing -> p95 error 1.1%      8.8% missing -> p95 error 2.2%
     17.5% missing -> p95 error 3.2%     21.3% missing -> p95 error 3.6%
     24.9% missing -> p95 error ~3.9%    33.7% missing -> p95 error 4.8%

So the frames admitted by the relaxed cut carry up to roughly 4% p95 error in
the extracted spectrum, against under 2% for the frames the strict cut allowed.
The QCMISS header keyword records each frames judged missing percentage; sort
on it to work with only the high-fidelity subset.

INTERPOLATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Normalised convolution followed by Laplace relaxation, using only surrounding
pixels, with radiation hits excluded as sources but left untouched in the data.
Only missing pixels are replaced; every other pixel is bit-identical to the raw
frame (verified for all 16 frames). Extension FILLMASK flags interpolated
pixels. Headers carry NFILLED, FILLFRAC, FILLMETH, FILLMAXD (whole frame),
FILLMAXS (science region) and QCMISS.

CAUTION: in NOPROC frames the fill is trustworthy only inside rows 187-286.
Frame 1140 fills an 80-row swath outside that band (FILLMAXD 83.4); such
regions are smooth interpolation across a large hole, not data.

The 65535 pixels are radiation hits and hot pixels, not saturated Jupiter
signal: their frequency is flat across the detector (7.6-8.6% per row band)
with no concentration at any spectral trace. They are left in place for the
usual cosmic-ray rejection downstream.
